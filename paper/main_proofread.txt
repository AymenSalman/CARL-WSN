Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) deployed in IoT-driven smart city
environments carry heterogeneous application traffic with fundamentally
different quality-of-service requirements. Existing hybrid routing
protocols switch between proactive and reactive modes based on a single
network metric, making them insensitive to application urgency and
unable to simultaneously optimise energy consumption and end-to-end
latency across different traffic classes. This paper proposes
CARHy-WSN, a Context-Aware Hybrid Routing protocol that introduces a
three-dimensional context vector - comprising application urgency
class, normalised residual energy, and link stability score - to
dynamically select the optimal routing mode per packet. CARHy-WSN is
implemented in MATLAB and evaluated against five baseline protocols
under three traffic classes and a mixed-traffic scenario in a 100-node
heterogeneous WSN simulation. Results demonstrate that CARHy-WSN
achieves an 82.5% reduction in Class A emergency packet latency
compared to AODV, a 70.2% reduction in routing overhead compared to
DSDV, and maintains a packet delivery ratio of 100% across all
evaluated scenarios, while preserving competitive energy balance
throughout all tested configurations. A lightweight decision tree
variant achieves 100% classification accuracy with only 0.3KB memory
overhead, enabling self-configuring deployment. CARHy-WSN prioritises
simultaneous latency and overhead optimisation for safety-critical
heterogeneous IoT deployments, achieving these gains within a
deliberate network lifetime trade-off relative to lifetime-optimised
baselines.

Introduction

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) have emerged as a
foundational component of Internet of Things (IoT)-driven smart city
infrastructure, enabling continuous and distributed monitoring across
domains including environmental sensing, smart transportation,
industrial automation, and emergency response .
As smart city deployments scale in both node density and application
diversity, the routing protocols governing data delivery face
increasing pressure to simultaneously satisfy conflicting requirements:
energy efficiency, low latency, reliability, and adaptability to
dynamic network conditions.

Existing routing approaches broadly fall into three categories.
Proactive protocols, such as DSDV  and
OLSR , maintain routing tables continuously,
delivering low latency at the cost of high energy overhead from
periodic updates. Reactive protocols, such as AODV  and
DSR , discover routes on demand, conserving
energy in sparse networks but introducing significant route
discovery delays that are intolerable
for time-critical applications. Hybrid protocols, such as
ZRP , partition the network into zones and apply
different modes within and across zone boundaries. However, ZRP and
its variants rely on a fixed zone radius parameter and remain
insensitive to the nature of the traffic being routed.

In a realistic smart city WSN, a single node may simultaneously
generate emergency alerts requiring sub-100ms delivery, periodic
telemetry with a 30-second tolerance, and event-driven uploads with
best-effort requirements. No existing protocol adapts its routing
behaviour to the urgency class of individual packets. This
application-blindness leads to systematic inefficiency: proactive
routing wastes energy on low-urgency telemetry, while reactive routing
introduces dangerous delays on emergency traffic.

A 2025 comprehensive survey of context-aware routing in
IoT-driven WSNs  explicitly identifies
dynamic application-context-based hybrid mode switching as
an open research direction, noting that reactive and hybrid
context-aware approaches have received significantly less
attention than proactive ones. No published protocol uses
a joint multi-dimensional context vector - comprising
application urgency, residual energy, and link stability
simultaneously - to trigger full routing paradigm switching.
This paper addresses that gap directly.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

    A three-dimensional context vector
     comprising application urgency
    class , normalised residual energy , and link stability
    score , and a mode-switching decision table that maps each
    context combination to the optimal routing mode.

    CARHy-WSN, the first WSN hybrid routing protocol that
    performs per-packet routing mode selection based on a joint
    three-dimensional context vector, requiring under 0.1ms
    classification latency and under 2KB RAM overhead per node.

    Evaluation under three simultaneous heterogeneous IoT
    traffic classes, providing the first routing comparison under
    realistic smart city mixed-traffic conditions.

    Validation that a lightweight decision tree classifier
(depth 3, 0.3KB memory, 7 comparisons per packet) learns
the context-switching rules with 100% test accuracy and
99.96% cross-validation accuracy, demonstrating the
feasibility of a self-configuring deployment that requires
no manual threshold tuning.

Related Work

Proactive and Reactive Routing Protocols

Routing protocols for WSNs and ad hoc networks have been broadly
classified into proactive and reactive categories, each with
distinct performance trade-offs that make neither approach
universally optimal for heterogeneous IoT deployments.

Routing protocols for WSNs have been extensively surveyed and
classified .
Proactive protocols maintain routing tables continuously through
periodic broadcasts. DSDV  established the
foundational destination-sequenced distance-vector approach,
guaranteeing loop-free routes at all times. OLSR extended this paradigm through multipoint relay (MPR) selection,
substantially reducing flooding overhead in dense networks.
While proactive protocols deliver consistently low latency by
eliminating route discovery delays, their continuous maintenance
overhead leads to significant energy waste on low-traffic
nodes .

Reactive protocols discover routes exclusively on demand.
AODV  pioneered this approach by initiating
route requests only when data must be transmitted, conserving
energy in sparse or low-traffic conditions. However, the
route discovery delay of 20-100ms introduced by RREQ/RREP
flooding renders reactive protocols unsuitable for
latency-sensitive applications .
A comparative analysis by Nurul Hidayah et al. confirmed that neither proactive nor reactive protocols alone
achieve optimal performance across varying network conditions,
motivating the need for adaptive hybrid approaches.
Recent work  demonstrated
that AODV, DSDV, and DSR all exhibit significant performance
degradation under heterogeneous traffic loads, highlighting
the fundamental limitation of single-mode routing for
IoT-driven smart city deployments.

Hybrid Routing Protocols

Hybrid protocols attempt to combine the latency advantages of
proactive routing with the energy efficiency of reactive routing.
ZRP  pioneered zone-based hybrid routing by
maintaining proactive routes within a fixed-radius zone and
using reactive inter-zone routing for destinations beyond the
zone boundary. While ZRP reduces control overhead compared to
purely proactive protocols, its fixed zone radius is configured
statically and does not adapt to node energy state or traffic
urgency - a fundamental limitation that CARHy-WSN addresses
directly.

Subsequent hybrid approaches incorporated additional optimisation
dimensions. Chaurasiya et al. proposed a hybrid clustering technique for multilevel heterogeneous
IoT WSNs, combining cluster-based organisation with multi-hop
routing to reduce energy consumption. Lei proposed a hybrid energy-aware approach combining particle swarm
optimisation with fuzzy clustering for IoT routing, demonstrating
52% energy reduction compared to DEEC, though at the cost of
requiring offline training data for the fuzzy membership functions.
Srinivasa Babu et al. proposed a neuro-fuzzy hybrid routing scheme for IoT that
incorporates context-like features, but similarly requires a
trained model that cannot adapt at runtime without retraining.
Nadanam et al.  recently proposed a
quantum-optimised hybrid routing framework achieving 30%
energy reduction, though the computational complexity of quantum
optimisation makes it unsuitable for resource-constrained sensor
hardware. Sudha et al.  combined
Q-learning with machine learning for cluster head selection and
routing in 5G-enabled WSNs, demonstrating improved throughput
over LEACH, but without considering per-packet application
urgency as a routing criterion.

A common limitation across all these hybrid approaches is that
mode selection or routing decisions are made based on a single
optimisation objective - typically energy or load - without
awareness of the application-level urgency of the traffic being
routed.

Context-Aware Routing in IoT-WSNs

Context-aware computing for IoT was formally defined by
Perera et al. , who identified five
contextual dimensions - identity, location, activity, time,
and relations - as fundamental to intelligent IoT decision
making. This foundational work motivates the use of contextual
information in routing decisions, though the specific application
to WSN hybrid routing mode selection had not been addressed
until recently.

Context-aware routing has since been applied across several IoT
and WSN domains. Monteiro et al. proposed context-aware network selection in heterogeneous wireless
networks, demonstrating that context-driven decisions outperform
static policies, but their work focused on network selection
rather than intra-WSN routing mode switching.
Akpakwu et al.  proposed a
context-aware congestion control scheme for CoAP/UDP IoT
networks, applying context sensing to transport-layer decisions
rather than routing.
Sithik and Kumar  proposed a
multi-context aware parent selection scheme for the RPL
protocol using grey set theory, incorporating multiple
contextual parameters into routing metric computation.
However, their approach targets RPL parent selection exclusively
and does not implement dynamic switching between fundamentally
different routing modes (proactive, reactive, and hybrid)
based on application urgency class. Unlike Sithik and
Kumar , who optimise a single
routing metric within RPL, CARHy-WSN introduces a
three-dimensional context vector that triggers full routing
paradigm switching - the first such mechanism reported in
the WSN routing literature.
Karagiannis et al. proposed context-aware routing for fog computing systems,
reducing latency by 23% and hop count by 73% through
context-driven forwarding decisions. Their work operates
at the fog layer rather than the WSN sensor layer, and
does not model heterogeneous traffic classes with
conflicting QoS requirements.
Hasan et al.  proposed a
context-aware trust prediction framework for opportunistic
IoT routing using Bayesian inference, achieving 88.9%
delivery ratio, though the trust-based context dimension
differs fundamentally from the urgency-energy-stability
context vector proposed in CARHy-WSN.

Smart city IoT routing has also received significant attention. Hanif et al.  proposed opportunistic IoT-WSN integration for smart city deployments.
Inga et al.  addressed WSN sizing and
routing for smart city deployments.
Ekler et al.  proposed energy-aware
IoT routing algorithms specifically for smart city environments.
Karunkuzhali et al. 
 proposed QoS-aware routing
frameworks for IoT-WSNs in smart cities, demonstrating that
multi-class service differentiation improves network performance.
However, none of these works implement dynamic routing mode
switching based on per-packet application urgency.
El-Fouly et al. proposed environment-aware real-time routing for multi-sink
WSNs in smart cities, incorporating deadline constraints and
link quality metrics. While this work shares the multi-sink
topology with our prior work  and considers
real-time delivery requirements, it does not model
heterogeneous application urgency classes or implement
context-driven mode switching.

Recent surveys confirm that AI-assisted and context-aware
routing are among the most active research frontiers in
IoT-WSN networking.
Priyadarshi  reviewed
meta-heuristic and AI-based routing approaches, while
Priyadarshi  further examined
AI-driven routing improvements for energy efficiency and
reliability.
Saadallah and Alabady identified energy-efficient context-sensitive routing as an
open challenge in their comprehensive IoT routing study.
Yilmaz et al.  highlighted the
integration of ML and AI into WSN routing as a key trend for
5G/6G sustainable communication.
Daanoune and Baghdad  recently
proposed a reinforcement learning-based clustering and routing
protocol (RLCR) for heterogeneous WSNs, demonstrating the
potential of AI-driven approaches for adaptive routing, though
without application-context awareness in mode selection.

Research Gaps

The preceding review reveals four specific gaps that motivate
CARHy-WSN. First, Khedr et al.  conducted
the first comprehensive survey of context-aware routing in
IoT-driven WSNs and explicitly identified dynamic
application-context-based mode switching as an open research
direction, noting that reactive and hybrid context-aware methods
have received significantly less attention than proactive
approaches. Second, Kumar et al. identified application-context awareness as an emerging
research gap through a bibliometric analysis of
energy-efficient routing in IoT WSNs spanning 2015-2024.
Third, Chu et al. ,
Khanna et al. , and
Prashanth et al.  all noted that routing
adaptation in heterogeneous WSNs remains dominated by
single-objective optimisation approaches that cannot
simultaneously satisfy the conflicting requirements of
heterogeneous application traffic classes.
Fourth, no published protocol uses a joint three-dimensional
context vector comprising application urgency, residual energy
ratio, and link stability score to dynamically select between
proactive, reactive, and hybrid routing modes on a per-packet
basis - the specific contribution of CARHy-WSN introduced
in this paper.

CARHy-WSN Protocol Design

Design Motivation

Existing hybrid routing protocols such as ZRP determine routing mode based on a single criterion - typically
geographic zone membership defined by a fixed hop-count radius.
This design is application-blind: a proactive route maintained for
a best-effort sensor reading consumes the same energy as one
maintained for an emergency alert, despite their vastly different
urgency levels. CARHy-WSN addresses this by introducing a
three-dimensional context vector that jointly considers
application urgency, node energy state, and link quality before
each routing decision.

Routing Modes

Proactive Mode
Inspired by DSDV , proactive mode maintains
routing tables through periodic broadcasts every round. When a
packet requires transmission, the next hop toward the BS is
retrieved immediately from the pre-built table - eliminating
route-discovery latency entirely. The energy overhead includes
both data transmission cost and a table maintenance overhead of
10% of the data packet energy per round. This mode is mandatory
for Class A emergency traffic.

Reactive Mode
Inspired by AODV , reactive mode initiates
route discovery only when a packet needs to be sent. A Route
Request (RREQ) is flooded to all neighbours within transmission
range, and a Route Reply (RREP) is returned along the reverse path.
This eliminates periodic broadcast overhead but introduces route
discovery delay of 20-50ms. Reactive mode is preferred for
Class B and C traffic on healthy nodes with stable links.

Hybrid Mode
CARHy-WSN's hybrid mode extends ZRP  with a
key innovation: the proactive zone radius adapts dynamically based
on the node's residual energy ratio :



Within the zone radius, proactive routing is used (routes
pre-built). Beyond the zone radius, reactive inter-zone routing
(IERP) is used. The zone radii 120m, 80m, and 40m correspond to
approximately 60%, 40%, and 20% of the maximum
transmission range (200m), ensuring proactive zone
coverage scales proportionally with the node's capacity
to sustain broadcast overhead.

is therefore a direct consequence of the lifetime trade-off, not
a routing efficiency deficiency.

Decision Tree Classifier Validation

To validate Contribution 4, a lightweight decision tree
classifier was trained on 10,000 synthetically generated
context vectors labelled by the rule-based oracle.
The feature vector comprises the three context dimensions
 and the target is one of three routing
modes proactive, reactive, hybrid.

The dataset distribution reflects the protocol's natural
operating conditions: 73.8% proactive (dominated by
Class A emergency traffic), 16.1% reactive, and 10.1%
hybrid. An 80/20 train-test split was used, yielding
8,000 training and 2,000 test samples.

The trained decision tree (7 leaves, 0.3KB memory,

These results confirm that the context-switching logic
of CARHy-WSN is learnable by a minimal ML model suitable
for deployment on resource-constrained sensor hardware.
A self-configuring variant could learn the threshold
parameters  and  from observed network behaviour
without manual configuration - an important practical
advantage for large-scale smart city deployments.
The computational cost of the decision tree (7 comparisons,
ms latency, 0.3KB RAM) is negligible compared
to the packet transmission and reception energy costs,
confirming its deployability on standard sensor hardware.


Discussion

Design Trade-offs

The most notable trade-off in CARHy-WSN is network
lifetime versus latency-overhead optimisation. EH-Routing
achieves a significantly longer network lifetime
(FND = 787 vs. CARHy FND = 34) because it always selects
the highest-residual-energy relay, concentrating traffic
load on energy-rich nodes and preserving the overall
network energy budget. CARHy-WSN does not optimise for
lifetime alone: it routes Class A traffic via proactive
mode regardless of relay energy state, and routes
Class B and C traffic via the energy-cheapest mode
given current context. This design is intentional -
in smart city deployments, a 47.5ms emergency response
latency (EH-Routing) is unacceptable for fire alarms or
accident detection, where the 100ms hard deadline
must be guaranteed. CARHy-WSN delivers 17.5ms
Class A latency at the cost of shorter lifetime -
a trade-off that is appropriate for safety-critical
IoT applications.

Future work can address this trade-off by incorporating
energy harvesting awareness into the context vector,
extending the  dimension with a harvesting rate
forecast - directly connecting CARHy-WSN to the
energy-harvesting routing work in .

The CARHy-WSN context classifier uses two threshold

accuracy) confirm that the switching boundaries learned
from these thresholds generalise well across unseen

Practical Deployment Considerations

CARHy-WSN requires each node to maintain an ACK history
buffer of  entries (80 bytes) and compute an
EWMA over this buffer per packet. The total per-node
memory overhead is under 100 bytes beyond the standard
routing table, making the protocol suitable for
deployment on widely available sensor platforms
including TelosB, MicaZ, and Arduino-class devices.
The proactive routing table is refreshed every round,
which in a 2000-round simulation corresponds to the
full network operational lifetime - in practice, the refresh interval should be aligned to
the network's expected round duration.

Regarding scalability, CARHy-WSN's context classifier
operates in  time per packet regardless of network
size , as urgency class  is read directly from the
packet header,  requires a single division, and

The proactive routing table scales  in memory, which
is standard for all proactive protocols and acceptable for
the node densities typical of smart city WSN deployments.
Critically, CARHy-WSN's overhead advantage over DSDV is
expected to increase with larger : as network density
grows, DSDV must broadcast routing updates to an increasing
number of neighbours every round, while CARHy-WSN suppresses
these broadcasts for Class B and C traffic by selecting
reactive mode - meaning the absolute overhead saving per
packet grows proportionally with neighbour density, amplifying

Limitations

This study has four limitations that should be
acknowledged. First, the simulation uses a simplified
propagation model (first-order radio energy model)
that does not capture multipath fading, shadowing,
or interference from co-deployed networks. Second,
node mobility is not modelled - all nodes are
static after deployment. Third, the latency values
are simulated rather than measured on physical
hardware. Fourth, all results are reported for a single
network size (); scalability to larger deployments
() is left for future validation.
Future work should validate CARHy-WSN
on a real sensor testbed (e.g. Raspberry Pi or
Arduino with IEEE 802.15.4 radios) to confirm that
the simulated latency advantages hold under realistic
channel conditions.


Conclusion

This paper presented CARHy-WSN, a Context-Aware Hybrid
Proactive-Reactive Routing Protocol for heterogeneous
IoT-driven Wireless Sensor Networks. CARHy-WSN introduces
a three-dimensional context vector  that jointly considers application urgency
class, normalised residual energy, and link stability
to dynamically select the optimal routing mode per
packet - the first such mechanism reported in the
WSN routing literature.

MATLAB-based simulation across six protocols, four
traffic scenarios, and five independent runs demonstrated
that CARHy-WSN achieves an 82.5% reduction
in Class A emergency packet latency compared to AODV
(), a 70.2% reduction in routing
overhead compared to DSDV (), and a
100% packet delivery ratio across all evaluated
scenarios. CARHy-WSN is the only evaluated protocol that simultaneously
achieves near-optimal emergency latency and minimum routing
overhead - a Pareto improvement over all five baselines in the
latency-overhead plane, including DSDV which matches
CARHy-WSN on latency but generates 70.2% higher routing
overhead, confirming that CARHy-WSN occupies the optimal
operating point on the latency-overhead trade-off frontier.
This improvement is achieved within a deliberate network
lifetime trade-off: CARHy-WSN prioritises sub-100ms
emergency response over maximum node survival, an appropriate
design choice for safety-critical IoT deployments. A lightweight
decision tree variant achieves 100% test accuracy
and 99.96% cross-validation accuracy with only
0.3KB memory overhead, confirming the feasibility of self-configuring deployment
in which sensor nodes autonomously learn optimal threshold
values  and  from observed network behaviour -
eliminating the need for manual parameter tuning during
installation of large-scale smart city sensor deployments.

Three directions for future work are identified.
First, integrating energy harvesting rate forecasting
into the context vector to address the network lifetime
trade-off demonstrated against EH-Routing .
Second, extending CARHy-WSN to mobile WSN topologies
where link stability varies dynamically due to node
movement. Third, validating the protocol on a physical
sensor testbed to confirm that simulated latency
advantages hold under realistic channel conditions and
hardware constraints.
